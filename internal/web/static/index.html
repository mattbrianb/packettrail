    Help

    Getting started

    PacketTrail shows which apps on this PC talk to which addresses on the network, how much data each one moves, and who owns those addresses.

    • Run it as administrator on Windows for byte counts, RTT and Loss ↓. Without admin rights it runs in connections only mode: you see who talks to whom, but not how much. The status pill at the top left shows the mode and offers Restart as administrator.
    • Live or history: Live shows this session. 1H to All read the stored history, which is kept for 90 days in the data folder next to the app. Every table and chart follows this choice.
    • Units and appearance: bps or B/s for rates, and the three icons for system, light or dark appearance.
    • Copying: every IP, hostname, prefix and ASN has a copy button, and each detail panel has Copy details. Tables don't refresh while you have text selected.
    • Exporting: Export CSV saves the current table, with your sort and search applied, to your Downloads folder.

    The views

    Applications
    Every app with traffic. Click an app to open its details on the right: its chart and every remote IP and port it used. Click it again, click anywhere outside the panel, or press Esc to close it.
    Remote IPs
    Every remote address, with location, network (ASN), hostname and path quality. Click a row to open its details in the panel on the right, with diagnostics. Click another row to switch, or anywhere outside the panel to close it. Hide LAN & private hides local addresses.
    Networks
    Traffic per network operator (ASN). Click one to see, on the right, which of its prefixes and IPs carry your traffic.
    Peering
    Your busiest networks matched against PeeringDB: their peering policy and the internet exchanges you share. Route map traces the path to your top 8 networks and shows where each one leaves your network.
    Monitors
    Continuous ping or TCP checks to hosts you choose, such as your gateway or DNS servers, with loss and latency history. They keep running while you use other tabs. Pause all stops them without losing history.
    Tools
    MTR, traceroute, ping or a TCP port check to any IP address or hostname, including ones this PC hasn't talked to.
    System
    CPU, memory, disks, network adapters, the TCP/IP stack and processes, with a list of things worth checking. Use it to rule the PC in or out when the network seems slow.

    Reading the numbers

    ↓ Rate, ↑ Rate
    Current download and upload speed.
    Total rate
    Download and upload combined. Click its header to sort by it: whatever is using the most bandwidth right now moves to the top as rates change, so you can catch a bandwidth hog in the act.
    Downloaded, Uploaded
    Data moved in the selected period. These count payload only, so they read a few percent below router or interface counters.
    RTT
    Average TCP round-trip time, measured by the operating system on your real connections. No probes are sent.
    Loss ↓ (est.)
    Download side: the share of received data where your PC noticed a gap, meaning packets were lost or reordered on the way to you. Yellow above 0.5%, red above 2%. Windows in admin mode only.
    Retrans ↑
    Upload side: the share of sent data TCP had to send again. Yellow above 1%, red above 5%.
    Port
    → 443 means this PC connected out to remote port 443. ← 3389 means another host connected in to local port 3389.
    Share
    On the Peering tab: a network's part of all the traffic this PC exchanged with networks outside your own (off-net) in the selected period. Your own network (on-net) and LAN traffic aren't counted. A high share with no shared exchange is where peering would help most.
    A dash (–)
    Nothing measured yet. It is not the same as zero.

    Diagnostics

    Open them from any IP's details panel, from the Tools tab, or with the MTR button on a monitor card.

    • MTR probes every hop each second and builds loss and latency statistics per hop. Loss at a middle hop that doesn't continue to the later hops usually just means that router answers probes slowly; it is not real loss.
    • Traceroute maps the path once.
    • Ping shows loss, latency and jitter. TCP port times the connection handshake instead, for hosts that block ping.
    • The path diagram above MTR and traceroute results groups hops by network: your LAN, your ISP, internet exchanges and the destination. Private addresses are your LAN only until the path leaves it; later ones are a provider's routers, shown inside the network around them or as Private IPs.
    • Copy results gives an mtr-style text report for tickets and chats. Monitor keeps testing the address in the background.
    • Up to four tests can run at once, and each stops after 15 minutes.

    What leaves this PC

    Location and network lookups use offline databases stored in the data folder. Only these go out:

    • Reverse-DNS queries to your normal DNS server.
    • Anonymous PeeringDB lookups by AS number, on the Peering tab.
    • The probes you start: diagnostics, monitors and the route map. ISP detection also sends a few pings towards 1.1.1.1, at most every 6 hours.
    • The free DB-IP Lite GeoIP databases: downloaded once on first start, then only when you click Download latest in Settings.

    History, settings and test results stay on this PC.

    Troubleshooting

    “Connections only · no byte counts”
    The app isn't running as administrator. Click Restart as administrator in the status pill.
    Windows SmartScreen or an antivirus warns about the app
    PacketTrail isn't code-signed, so Windows can't show a verified publisher. On a new PC, SmartScreen may say “Windows protected your PC”: click More info, then Run anyway. If an antivirus quarantines it, restore the file and report it as a false positive to that vendor. Only allow it if you got the file from someone you trust. Compare its SHA-256 with SHA256SUMS.txt to be sure it's unchanged.
    RTT or Loss ↓ show dashes
    They need admin rights, and they only exist for TCP. Browsers often use QUIC (UDP) for Google and YouTube, which has no TCP figures.
    Peering says “Looking up…”
    PeeringDB limits anonymous lookups, so results arrive in batches every few seconds. The note above the table says when PeeringDB asked the app to wait.
    An app shows no traffic
    Check the period: Live only covers this session. Apps that only talk to this PC itself are hidden unless the app was started with --loopback.
    The network feels slow but the line looks fine
    Open the System tab. High DPC or interrupt time, a pinned CPU core, adapter errors, or Ethernet stuck at 100 Mbit/s all point at the PC rather than the network.
